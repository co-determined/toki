Tags: **C** = confirmed against the source text (URL given). **C2** = confirmed only through a secondary source quoting a primary one (usually Muppet Wiki, a fan wiki). **U** = unconfirmed.

## 0. Is there already an AI or chatbot Sesame puppet? As far as I can find, no.
- **C** "AI and Us" (Sesame Workshop video, 14 Oct 2025): Julia's family uses an AI. Sesame's note says: *"Julia and her family use an AI device… the one shown here is purely fictional."* The device is a tool, not a character. https://sesameworkshop.org/resources/ai-and-us/ ToughPigs says the device is called "Reiko" and reads the video as sponsor-friendly to Google: https://www.toughpigs.com/sesame-ai-julia/
- **C2** Norbert the Robot (2017, Ep. 4709, Martin P. Robinson) works at Hooper's and *"maintain[s] a database on Sesame Street residents."* He is an appliance-robot, not an AI. https://muppet.fandom.com/wiki/Norbert_the_Robot
- **C2** Smartie (2017) is an animated talking smartphone in Elmo's World, used to play video clips. https://muppet.fandom.com/wiki/Smartie
- **C** *Mecha Builders* (CG spinoff): Elmo, Cookie, Abby "as robot heroes-in-training." https://sesameworkshop.org/about-us/press-room/sesame-street-mecha-builders/
- Instrument warning: a search summary credited Sesame with "AI is… like having a smart friend." It's a fan Substack (fazli.substack.com), not Sesame.
- **U**: I can't prove a negative. International co-productions and Netflix Season 56 weren't checked character by character.

## 1. Robots and computers in Henson and Sesame
**Sam the Robot ("Super-Automated Machine")**
- **C** Henson archivist on the Red Book post "8/31/1972 – 'Robot due on Sesame Street'": Sam *"was supposed to be infallible but, of course, was constantly confused and made mistakes."* Jerry Nelson performed him. Jim Henson drew the design sketches in 1972. The post also says: *"a robot or machine that made mistakes helped underline the essential value of man's humanity… gave children the opportunity gain confidence from knowing that they knew more than a seemingly sophisticated machine."* https://web.archive.org/web/20260228022910/https://www.henson.com/jimsredbook/2011/08/8311972/ (the live henson.com page returns 403)
- **C2** Sam ran seasons 4–10 (1972–79). His gag: stuck repeating words until someone whacked him. Bow tie; gadgets (dial, screen, faucet, gumball machine). Spinney also performed him. Writer Norman Stiles: *"Most of the writers had a hard time coming up with ideas for him… the character we created didn't have legs."* Jerry Nelson wanted Sam's screen to "segue into animation." https://muppet.fandom.com/wiki/Sam_the_Robot

**The Paperwork Explosion (1967)**
- **C** It was an IBM film promoting the MT/ST, a Selectric typewriter with magnetic tape. Score by Raymond Scott. Tagline: **"Machines should work. People should think."** About five minutes long. https://west86th.bgc.bard.edu/articles/paperwork-explosion/ and https://slate.com/culture/2012/11/ben-kafkas-the-demon-of-writing-reviewed.html (Slate calls it "a subversive poem about… the mechanization of the mind.")

## 2. Construction
- **C, hand-rod (Smithsonian NMAH):** *"the performer's dominant hand goes into the puppet's head and operates the mouth… The less dominant hand controls the arm rods, which are thin rods connected to the puppet's hands."* Bert is hand-rod.
  **Live-hand:** one hand is in the head and the other is in the puppet's gloved arm. A second puppeteer "right-hands." Ernie and Oscar are live-hand. https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_1448973 · nmah_1448974 · nmah_1182904
- **C** Elmo is hand-rod (Mental Floss 2024; arm rod visible in ref 01): https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/how-sesame-street-puppeteers-control-muppets
- **C, measured whole puppets (NMAH), not heads:**

  | Bert (1969 pilot) | 23½×23×9 in | foam, felt, plastic, fabric | |
  | Ernie (1969 pilot) | 22×19×14 in | foam, felt, plastic, fabric | |
  | Oscar (1970s) | 36×24×12 in | | |

  https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_1444757
- **U: head size alone.** No published head-only measurement found.
- **C, mouth (kids' craft book by the Muppet Workshop):** "move your thumb up and down to operate the puppet's lower jaw." *The Muppets Make Puppets!* (1994), searched inside: https://archive.org/details/muppetsmakepuppe00hens
- **U:** professional Sesame mouth-plate build (material, hinge, finger sleeves); only hobbyist guides found.
- **C, monitors:** MoMI label: Spinney worked Big Bird "while watching a small monitor strapped to a harness" (ref 06).
- **C, Antron fleece** (Georgia Stage, maker of the successor): nylon fleece from Larry Guild's Hampshire Textiles, originally with DuPont Antron fiber. Henson's first order (1970s trade show): "Miss Piggy Pink" and "Kermit the Frog Green." Now "no longer being manufactured." https://gastage.com/content/24-antron-fleece-faq
  Note: Elmo is **fur**, not fleece. The 1969 Bert and Ernie predate Antron.
- **C2, Henson stitch:** credited to Don Sahlin. It sews cloth as tightly as possible so seams are "nearly invisible." https://muppet.fandom.com/wiki/Don_Sahlin. I found no primary source.

**Eyes and focus (strongest material)**
- **C** *Jim Henson's Designs and Doodles* (2001), searched inside: Sahlin's "Magic Triangle" *"refers to the area between the focal point of the eyes in relation to the nose and mouth. Don thought that the eyes had to be positioned in exactly the right way, otherwise the character wouldn't come to life."* Henson: *"It would be the last thing he would do, and he always wanted me there… making sure the eyes had a point of focus, because without that you had no character."* https://archive.org/details/jimhensonsdesign0000inch
- **C** *The Muppets Make Puppets!*: *"Muppets usually are slightly cross-eyed; this makes them appear to be looking at someone or something not too far…"* (snippet cut off there). https://archive.org/details/muppetsmakepuppe00hens
- **C** Sesame newsletter, 2006: *"The smaller the pupil, the older the Muppet looks; the larger the pupil, the younger the Muppet looks."* https://web.archive.org/web/20071204044638/https://www.sesameworkshop.org/aboutus/newsletter_article.php?contentId=15860086
- **C2** Snuffy's 1971 eyes (bulging, yellow-green) were judged frightening and replaced with round white eyes. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Snuffleupagus

**Anything Muppets and Whatnots**
- **C2** Blank heads; features, bodies, clothes added (swapped on camera in season 1). Ed Christie: *"Jim Henson developed artificial colors or strange colors for all the humanoids"* so they wouldn't be race-specific. https://muppet.fandom.com/wiki/Anything_Muppets

**Builders' quotes:** only Henson, Sahlin ("Guardian of the Essence," per *Designs and Doodles*) and Krewson (§3). **U:** nothing citable from Erickson, Love, Frith or Wilcox.

## 3. Curriculum-driven Muppets
**Julia**
- **C** CBS *60 Minutes* (19 Mar 2017). Rollie Krewson: *"when she gets upset, she flaps her hands. So she has two separate sets of arms… they would switch on set."*
  Krewson: *"Her eyes had to be a certain way because she has to have an intense look, but she has to look friendly."* No hair adornments; bangs off her eyes; loose purple dress; **half-closed and fully closed eye sets** for when she's overwhelmed. Sesame worked with "autism organizations." https://www.cbsnews.com/news/julia-first-sesame-street-muppet-with-autism · https://www.cbsnews.com/news/julia-sesame-street-new-muppet-autism/
- **C** Smithsonian (Suskind, Dec 2017): she was made a girl "after much debate" ("counterintuitive"). Designer Louis Mitchell. https://www.smithsonianmag.com/innovation/team-sesame-street-created-character-autism-180967218/
- **C2** ASAN consulted early; cut ties in 2019 over Autism Speaks PSAs. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_(Sesame_Street)
- **U:** a stated design rule that she avoids eye contact. In the scripts she just doesn't answer Big Bird.

**Kami** (Takalani Sesame, 2002)
- **C** (read through a fetch summarizer, not raw text) TIME, Sept 2002: "golden-yellow"; age five; *"insight that goes beyond her five years."* https://time.com/archive/6893358/positively-sesame-street/
- **C2** She is HIV-positive. Her name comes from *Kamogelo*, "acceptance." Designer Ed Christie.

**Lily**
- **C** CNN, 2018: *"a 7-year-old bright pink Muppet."* She was introduced in 2011 for food insecurity and returned in 2018 for homelessness. https://www.cnn.com/2018/12/12/health/sesame-street-homeless-muppet-bn

**Ji-Young** (2021)
- **C** AP via NPR: 7, Korean American, electric guitar, skateboard. Name: "smart/wise… brave"; *"Ji also means sesame."* Kathleen Kim: not *"generically pan-Asian."* Outside experts plus an internal "culture trust"; built "in a matter of months" (usually "a couple of years"). https://www.npr.org/2021/11/15/1055733980/

**Snuffy reveal** (aired 18 Nov 1985)
- **C** Norman Stiles: *"letters from people who worked with children who had experienced some kind of abuse… they often don't think they'll be believed."* Carol-Lynn Parente: *"if we represented adults not believing what kids said, they might not be motivated to tell the truth."* https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/the-office/oral-history-1985-mr-snuffleupagus-shocked-sesame-street

## 4. Ed Palmer's distractor
**C** (*"G" is for Growing*, 2001, searched inside: https://archive.org/details/gisforgrowingthi00shal)

Children faced a screen of *Sesame Street* and *"a second screen that simultaneously showed unrelated slides, as researchers continuously noted the screen to which children attended."* Results were drawn as graphs *"approximately 10 feet long, with a data point every 7.5 seconds… like a stock market graph."* Segments that lost children were cut or reworked.

The book also says: *"It was researchers who suggested the inclusion of Muppets in live-action Street scenes, but it was production people who created the specific characters of Big Bird and Oscar."*

**C2** Air/rework thresholds (80–90% / 50%): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesame_Street_research
