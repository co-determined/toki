# Crit: the puppeteer's seat

## 1. Cold (written before opening the bible)

**Who.** A speech balloon that learned to talk: the shape of a word with nobody's words in it yet. It reads young (high, close eyes, big pupils), eager, a little anxious. The curl puts its resting state at *not knowing*. The balloon's tail runs down into my arm, so the speaker is under the playboard, and the shape admits it. I read it as the AI before anyone told me.

**What I can do with it.** The flat top and the piped seam work like a spirit level: every degree of roll reads, so a thinking tilt, doubt, or leaning toward a child costs me almost nothing. The mouth runs the full width of the head, so a laugh will reach the back row. And 555 g is a weight I can carry.

**What worries me.** That's a 46 cm head on one hand. Rotational inertia goes with width squared, so it has about 2.7 times the turning inertia of a 28 cm head of the same mass. Every quick look overshoots, and then the curl whips. The whole lower half of the head is the jaw. It's heavy and it hangs open under gravity, so keeping the mouth closed is a squeeze I hold all day. The arms come out from under the head, so any gesture above the face has to climb the head's sides. And the sleeve is cut at 25°, which keeps me on one side of the puppet for every setup.

## 2. After the bible

**Listening drives every segment**, not just beat 10, so holding the jaw shut is most of my day. **The catch is the catchphrase.** I'll do it forty times a day, so it has to live in the thumb and wrist, which repeat exactly.

The bible calls focus "a claim," but `src/maquette.py` aims the pupils fresh every beat, separately from the head. Felt pupils go where the head goes. Measured against the build focus (0, 6, 70), beats 02 and 06 are off by 30–34°, more than the pupil's own 28.6° half-width, and 03/05/07/08 are off by 14–18°. Only 01, 04 and 10 are honest. Toki looks at something only by turning toward it, so draw it that way.

## 3. The ten beats

- **Hit as drawn: 01, 04, 10.** On 01, open on every syllable, and wave at shoulder height; the arms are only 24 cm.
- **Hit, with more head: 02, 05, 08.** 02 needs about 40° of pitch. On 05, Cookie has to hold the cookie an arm's length off his face, or looking at the cookie and looking at Cookie are the same look.
- **Re-stage: 03 and 07.** Hands overhead read as "ta-da." Play the catch this way: the thumb snaps the mouth shut mid-word, the head stops, a slow roll. On 07, square up to the child and drop the raised finger; it needs a swap hand and reads as a scold.
- **Can't: 06, and the eyes on 09.** Looking 34° lower means pitching about 50°, and the camera gets the top of a cushion. Have Elmo hold the notebook at 70 cm, where the focus is set. On 09, there's no laughing lid, and a laugh won't wait for a swap.
- **10 shouldn't be "perfectly still."** Still reads as a puppet set down. Listen with a small rise on the speaker's stresses, a nod, and focus moving between their face and their hands.

## 4. What the build makes hard

- **Weight:** 555 g is fine; 46 cm of width isn't. Quick takes overshoot, and the catchphrase is a take.
- **Tail:** a vertical arm rests, bone on bone. At 25° my shoulder holds the weight all day, and Toki can only lean in toward partners on camera-left.
- **Mouth plate:** about 40 × 17 cm of thin PETG on a 9 cm hand, so the corners will trail. My arm comes up through the jaw's floor; check that slot at the first fitting.
- **No nose:** turning left and right reads fine (Kermit). Looking down costs, because it shows the top of the head. Stage things at Toki's eye line.
- **Rods** cross the cream body in 05, 08 and 09. **Lids:** no blink and no change mid-take. The sheet's 0.05, 0.12 and 0.82 aren't sets that exist.

## 5. A beat you haven't drawn: Toki as Elmo's speech balloon

I rise behind Elmo with the tail aimed at his mouth, and the 25° lean becomes the comic-strip angle. Toki says what Elmo meant, slightly wrong, and Elmo fixes it. That's the bible's origin story and its engine in one picture, for the cost of half an apple box.

## 6. The one change

**Balance the jaw shut.** Put elastic across the hinge, tuned to cancel the lower jaw's weight, so a relaxed hand reads as a closed mouth. Listening stops costing me, and the catch gets its snap.

*— Thumb. Focus decides where it looks; the thumb decides when it talks.*
