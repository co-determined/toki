# Builder's crit: TOKI
*One seat: whoever makes it out of foam and fleece by Thursday. Measurements are from your sheets.*

## 1. Cold read

A word balloon that talks. The head is a cream box cushion, 46 × 33 × 22 cm and nearly all mouth, with navy piping. The tail is the sleeve, there's a question-mark curl, and the arms are on rods. The silhouette is strong and the construction is ordinary shop work. But the sheets disagree with each other, so I can't build from them yet. My first questions:
- **Where are the mouth corners?** The model sheet hinges a clamshell at the back. Pattern A's slash stops ~4 cm short of the side seams. Those are two different puppets.
- **Where does my arm go in?** The profile puts the forearm mid-head and draws a hand ~10 cm long. Real hands are 17–19 cm. With the wrist 11 cm behind the face, the fingertips come out through it.
- **What plane is the curl in?** It's widest in three-quarter view and still a hook in profile. There's no top view.

## 2. After the bible

*"The curl is the hook and Toki is the dot"* makes the curl the character. Beat 10's "perfectly still" makes weight a performance problem. `research.md` line 37 lists the mouth-plate build as unknown, so PETG and gaffer are guesses.

## 3. Most likely to go wrong

**1. The mouth, at first fitting.**
- The hand doesn't fit.
- At beat 09's 32°, the clamshell's sides gape ~11 cm at the front, so the velvet needs side gussets.
- E1/E2 are bowls under a flat smile. At 18 cm off-center they're 8 cm short of the lip, so the corners will flap.
- Gaffer creeps under lights and sweat. Stitch the hinge through drilled plates.

**2. Weight, by day three.** E1 is ~520 cm², about 105 g per plate in 1/16" PETG. The budget gives 70 g to plates, velvet *and* tongue together. The 60 g for the eyes assumes ~1.5 mm dome walls, which crack on the first playboard hit. Cement, magnets, lid caps and hand cores aren't budgeted. I'd guess 750–800 g, not 555.

**3. The curl, in week one.** The crew *will* hang Toki by the hook. Aluminum wire in half-inch foam pulls out or snaps, and reshaping it between takes speeds that up. Use a steel stem screwed to a plate inside the shell.

## 4. The builder's sheet

- **A** is the silhouette plus allowance: 52 × 40 cm on the fine grid, which I checked is the 5 cm one. To reach mid-depth it needs ~70 × 57, so A and B make a pillow. Pattern the skin off the carved foam. No sleeve opening is marked.
- **B tail + C** taper to a point, and their edges don't match (~20–36 cm against 47). The bottom opening is under 9 cm around; an elbow needs 30+.
- **D:** a 4 cm strip over 3/8" cord in pile leaves ~1 mm of allowance, so cut it at 6 cm. It crosses the mouth twice, which puts four cord ends on a moving lip.
- **Touching eyes** leave no room for two lid edges. Bridge the lids, or gap the eyes 8 mm.
- **Rare-earth magnets** on a puppet that reaches for children break your own rule: "nothing for a child to find."
- **Screw-on rods** back out when the rod is rolled. Use a swivel with a detent pin.
- **Hands** have no core, but beat 07 points a finger. **G** makes a ~2 cm arm; the model sheet draws ~6.

What I'd defend to my shop lead: the foam and fleece, a weight budget that says "weigh at first fitting," the tail angled toward the performer's shoulder, eyes going in last, and swapping lids instead of building a mechanism.

## 5. The one change before cutting fabric

**Move the arm to the back.** Put the sleeve directly under the back hinge, so the wrist *is* the hinge. The ~19 cm interior then takes a 17 cm hand flat, or a 19 cm hand slightly curled, and the jaw pivots where the hand does. Draw it as the missing top view, with the performer's hand traced in. Then sew a muslin.

— **Muslin**, builder's seat, 2026-10-18. A muslin is the cheap test piece you sew before you cut the good cloth. That's my recommendation, and my name.
